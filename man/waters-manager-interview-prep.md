### B. Delivery & Execution

**Q6. Engineer unavailable mid-sprint — how do you handle it?**

**Anchor: Assess → Redistribute → Rebalance → Communicate → Learn**

> *Situation:* Mid-sprint, an engineer owning a critical payment retry integration took 5 days unplanned leave (family emergency).
>
> *Actions:*
> 1. **Assess** — reviewed pending work within hours, built a risk log
> 2. **Redistribute** — picked an engineer with partial context, paired for 2 hours to bootstrap
> 3. **Rebalance** — descoped one low-priority story with PO *before* standup
> 4. **Communicate** — informed manager and stakeholders same day, revised expectations
> 5. **Protect the engineer** — ensured they weren't disturbed on leave
>
> *Result:* Sprint scope delivered on time. Retro action: **no critical story owned by a single engineer without a paired reviewer.**

**Q7. Aggressive deadline?**
> Data-driven trade-off. I present **three options** — full scope with new date, MVP on original date, or phased release. **I don't say no; I show the trade-off.** Stakeholder picks with full info. Documented decision.

**Q8. Tech debt vs features?**
> **80/20 rule** — 80% features, 20% tech debt each sprint. Tech debt tracked as first-class backlog stories with business impact. Big feature ships → schedule a debt-cleanup sprint. **Debt is not "extra work" — it's a delivery risk.**

**Q9. Sprint slipping mid-way?**
> Assess (blocker, scope, dependency?) → Descope with PO — **always ship something, never ship nothing** → Escalate early if it's a dependency. **Raise flags on Wednesday, not Friday.**

**Q10. Running a retro?**
> **Start / Stop / Continue** format, 45 min, team-led. Every retro must end with **2–3 concrete action items with owners and dates**. I track them in a running doc so we see progress over time.

### C. People & Team

**Q11. Handling an underperformer?**
> Three phases. **1** — private 1:1 to understand: is it skill, motivation, or personal? **2** — clear measurable expectations for 30-60-90 days, weekly check-ins. **3** — if no improvement, honest conversation with HR partner about role fit. **Fair, documented, no surprises.** Most people just need clarity + support, not exit.

**Q12. Giving hard feedback?**
> Private + specific + behavioral. **Model: Behavior → Impact → Expectation.**
> Example: "In yesterday's design review you interrupted twice → the reviewer disengaged → next time, let them finish, then respond."
> Never in public. Never about personality. Always about behavior + impact.

**Q13. Top performer wants promotion?**
> Two conversations. **1** — is it justified against role expectations (not tenure)? **2** — if yes, build a case with evidence and champion them upward. If not yet, show them the exact gap + timeline. **Never say "wait one more year" with no plan** — that's how you lose your best people.

**Q14. Onboarding a new engineer?**
> **30-60-90.** 30 — shadow, read docs, ship a small bug fix. 60 — own a small feature end-to-end. 90 — own a sprint story independently. **Buddy assigned, weekly 1:1s, on-call rotation only after 60 days.**

**Q15. Motivating a team?**
> Three levers: **1. Clarity** — everyone knows what they own and why. **2. Growth** — visible learning path, stretch stories. **3. Recognition** — public credit for wins, private feedback for gaps. **Autonomy + purpose + growth beats any pep talk.**

### D. Conflict & Stakeholders

**Q16. Conflict with a stakeholder?**
> PO wanted a feature in 2 sprints; team estimated 4. I presented three options with data (full/MVP/phased). PO picked MVP. Delivered on time, phase 2 in next quarter. **Lesson: never say no, offer trade-offs.**

**Q17. Conflict within the team?**
> Two engineers disagreed on microservice boundaries. I didn't take sides. **Framework: put both options on paper → weigh trade-offs (perf, testability, deployability) → agree on decision criteria first → then decide.** They picked option 2 together. **Rule: never let technical disagreements become personal.**

**Q18. Disagreement with your own manager?**
> My manager wanted to skip tests to hit a date. **Approach:** raised it in 1:1 (not in front of team), showed data — recent incidents caused by untested code, cost of hotfixes vs. cost of tests. He agreed to a middle ground. **Disagree privately, commit publicly.**

**Q19. Saying no to a stakeholder?**
> I don't say no — I say "**yes, and here's what we'd trade off.**" Turn it into a decision they own. If they still push, escalate transparently. **My job isn't to be liked; it's to protect team commitment and quality.**

### E. Technical Judgment

**Q20. Architecture decisions?**
> **Framework: Options → Trade-offs → Criteria → Decision → Reversibility.** I never fall in love with one solution. 2–3 options on paper, list pros/cons, pick decision criteria (cost, scale, team skill, time), decide, note if reversible or one-way. **One-way decisions get more scrutiny.**

**Q21. Buy vs Build?**
> Three questions. **Is it our core differentiator? Do we have the skill? What's the TCO over 3 years?** Not core → buy. Core but no skill → buy + build skill. Core + have skill → build. **Rule: don't build what a $500/month SaaS solves.**

**Q22. Production incident?**
> **Framework: Detect → Triage → Communicate → Fix → RCA → Prevent.** Detect via monitoring + alerts. Triage — severity, blast radius. Comms — status every 15 min. Fix — rollback first if possible, else hotfix. **Post-incident: blameless RCA with action items.**

**Q23. Monolith vs microservices?**
> **Depends on team, scale, and boundaries.** Start with a monolith if unclear. Move to microservices when you have (a) clear domain boundaries, (b) different scaling needs, (c) team size that justifies it. **Microservices solve organizational scale problems, not just technical ones.** Wrong choice = distributed monolith.

**Q24. Ensuring code quality?**
> Four things. **1. Standards** — coding guidelines, linters, formatters as code. **2. Reviews** — every PR reviewed, no self-merge to main. **3. Automation** — CI runs tests + SonarQube + security scan. **4. Culture** — retros surface quality issues, never punish reporting bugs.

**Q25. AI in eCommerce engineering?**
> Two lenses. **For developers** — productivity multiplier: code gen, code review, test gen, incident diagnosis. **For product** — personalization, search relevance, chatbots, content gen are changing customer experience. **A Manager's job: adopt thoughtfully — pilot small, measure impact, scale what works.**

## 7. Follow-Up Prep for Intro Claims

Since the intro mentions specific work, be ready for probing:

**"Which external systems did the microservices integrate with?"**
> For Stock — internal warehouse management via Kafka events. For Delivery Promise — external logistics providers' REST APIs (cut-off times, shipping estimates). For Notifications — third-party email and SMS providers for order confirmations and status updates.

**"Why microservices instead of Hybris extensions?"**
> Monolith deployments were risky and slow. These capabilities needed **independent scaling** — Stock has real-time checks, Delivery Promise has heavy computation, Notifications is high-throughput async. Separating them enabled independent deployability and scale.

**"How did microservices talk to Hybris?"**
> Hybris published events to Kafka; services consumed and acted. For sync calls, they used **OCC REST APIs**.

**"Data consistency across services?"**
> Eventual consistency via Kafka events. For critical flows like stock reservation — **idempotency keys and compensating transactions (SAGA pattern)**.

**"What is OCC?"**
> Omni Commerce Connect — Hybris's REST API framework. Exposes commerce capabilities (cart, catalog, checkout, user) as JSON APIs. Enables headless and multi-channel commerce.

**"Have you worked on B2B?"**
> My core experience is B2C at scale. I have a **strong understanding of B2B concepts** — org units, approval workflows, contract pricing, punch-out — and can ramp up quickly. The core Hybris framework is the same; B2B extends it with org hierarchy and approval flows.
