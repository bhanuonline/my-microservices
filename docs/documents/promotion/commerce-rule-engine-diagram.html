Layer 2 & 3 — Commerce & Promotion Engine

ruleengineservices + promotionengineservices — the types above the raw Drools engine

What are these extensions & why do they exist?

ruleengineservices

Provides the generic building blocks to create any kind of rule — not just promotions.

  • AbstractRule / SourceRule — "A rule has a code, dates, priority, conditions, actions"
  • RuleGroup — "Rules can be grouped"
  • Definitions — "Here are the available conditions & actions for the rule builder UI"
  • Templates — "Here are pre-made blueprints"

Without this: no way to define rules at all. No conditions, no actions, no rule builder in Backoffice.

promotionengineservices

Takes those generic blocks and makes them specifically about promotions.

  • PromotionSourceRule — "A rule belongs to a store (PromotionGroup)"
  • RuleBasedPromotion — "When published, create a promotion object"
  • PromotionActions — "How to adjust the cart (discount, free gift, delivery)"
  • Junction tables — "Which products/categories are in which rules?"

Without this: the engine can run rules, but wouldn't know how to apply a discount to a cart.

Analogy: Blank form builder + Specific "Create a Discount" form = Promotion rule system

The big picture:

PromotionSourceRule is the star. It's what business users create. It inherits scheduling (dates, priority, status) from AbstractRule and rule logic (conditions JSON, actions JSON) from SourceRule, then adds store scoping (PromotionGroup).

When published, it auto-generates a DroolsRule (Layer 1) and a RuleBasedPromotion (Layer 3). When the engine fires, it creates PromotionAction records on the order (adjust total, adjust entry, add product, change delivery).

The Definitions are the building blocks in the rule builder UI — they tell Backoffice what conditions and actions are available. Your LMG team extended this vocabulary with concept, brand, device, and card BIN conditions.
